mintages, and historical themes across pre-Federation, decimal, and

Victoria produces about half of all local items, with big cities like Melbourne and Canberra acting as the main hubs. Internationally, Northern Europe sends the most items directly to Canberra.

Together, these maps show how Australia relied on local cities for home-grown production while looking to Europe for imported historical objects.

02 · WHAT THE COLLECTION HOLDS, FROM MATERIALS TO THEMES

COLLECTION AND COMMEMORATIVE PROFILES

Medals and Other are the Main Collection

Medals and other heritage objects such as badges, pins, and ribbons, make up two-thirds of the collection because they were kept to remember historical events. Metals make coins last longer, while paper and plastics were chosen for high-turnover items like banknotes and stamps.

Official issues focus on military history and royalty because state mints aimed to project national authority. However, the museum's broader variety was shaped by private collectors like Timothy Millett and Cecil Ballard Jnr because public archives needed private contributions to capture everyday culture.

03 · HOW COLLECTING HAS CHANGED ACROSS AUSTRALIAN HISTORY

CHANGE ACROSS TIME AND ERAS

Old tokens show up early in the 1800s but disappear because people stopped using them and switched to regular coins. Meanwhile, medals last across many years because of its nature such as very strong and does not break easily.


Newer collections focus on banknotes and coins because paper money and special coins became very popular later on. However, stamp collections stop around the late 1900s because people stopped sending letters in the mail.

Collecting Surges Spike Around 1935, 1960 and 1987

Early items focused mostly on Royalty and Military themes because older coins and stamps were used to show government power and war history. Modern themes like First Nations and Wildlife only appear in recent years because Australia’s values shifted toward celebrating local culture, nature, and community.


Before 1901, a shortage of official coins led local shop owners to produce copper tokens, which made up over 60% of early collections. After the Australian government introduced official currency, private tokens production declined sharply.

First Nations and Wildlife Themes are Recent

Early Tokens Give Way to Medals and Other Collectibles

The "Other" category ranked first from 1966 onward, reaching nearly 60% of modern collections. This reflects a shift towards collecting more everyday items, such as badges, pins, and ribbons, rather than focusing mainly on traditional currency.

04 · HOW COIN MINTAGE VARIES BY THEME AND OVER TIME.

MINTAGE AND THEME

Commemorative Coin Mintage Has Fallen Since 1970

Coin production dropped steadily between 1970 and 2024 because official mints stopped making huge batches for everyday money and started making smaller, special sets just for collectors. In the past, single issues reached massive numbers, such as 49.6 million war coins in 1982 and 43.1 million royalty coins in 2001.

Overall, Australian commemorative coins have shifted from mass production to more targeted collector releases. Production is lower today, while themes are more diverse and culturally representative, showing greater focus on specific themes and collector interest.
